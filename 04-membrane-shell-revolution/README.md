# 04 · Membrane Analysis of a Shell of Revolution

Analytical **membrane (shell) theory** solution for an **axisymmetric
paraboloidal dome** under **self-weight**, implemented in both **Python** and
**MATLAB**, and intended for comparison with a finite-element model.

---

## Physics / method

The shell is generated by rotating the parabola `z = a·r²` about its axis.
Using the classical membrane theory for shells of revolution (Timoshenko &
Woinowsky-Krieger, *Theory of Plates and Shells*, Ch. 15):

* **Vertical equilibrium of the cap** above radius r →
  `N_φ = − W(r) / (2·π·r·sin φ)`
* **Equilibrium normal to the surface** →
  `N_φ/R₁ + N_θ/R₂ = −Z`,  `Z = q₀·cos φ`

giving the meridional force `N_φ`, the hoop force `N_θ` and the membrane
stresses `σ_φ = N_φ/t`, `σ_θ = N_θ/t` along the meridian. Results are only
reported away from the support edge, where membrane theory is valid and
bending "edge disturbance" is absent.

Two material/geometry variants:

| Variant | Domain |
|---|---|
| **steel** | Large, thin dome (script: `project2_membrane_dome_steel.py`) |
| **concrete** | Smaller, thicker dome (MATLAB: `project2_membrane_dome.m`) |

forces (`SF1`, `SF2`) at the comparison point `r/R = 0.6`.

---

## Results

* `N_φ` is compressive everywhere under self-weight; `N_θ` transitions from
  compression near the apex to tension lower down (classic dome behaviour).
* Both `N_φ` and `N_θ` stay finite at the apex via the analytic limit.
* Edge-region values (near `r/R → 1`) are excluded from the comparison
  because bending boundary layers dominate there.
